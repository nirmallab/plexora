<PluginBadge name="Thresholding" />

Thresholding sets a lower and an upper bound on one marker's intensity at a
time. Cells whose value for that marker falls between the two are drawn over
the image, so you can see the gate against the tissue as you move it.

## When to use it

Reach for this tool when you want to call a marker positive or negative by
eye — checking a stain, finding a cutoff for a single marker, or looking at
which cells a threshold you already have actually selects on this sample.
It works on one marker at a time; it does not combine markers or cluster
cells.

## Requirements

Thresholding needs a cell table whose marker columns have been told apart
from its metadata columns (the marker/metadata split, done once when the
table is added), and columns for a cell id, X, Y and an image id — Plexora
asks which column plays each role the first time it needs to know. A
segmentation mask is optional: gating draws cells from centroids when there
is no mask, and from the mask's outlines or fills when there is one.

## Opening it

With a project open, choose **Tools → Thresholding**, or press **Ctrl+B**
(**⌘B** on macOS).

## Core workflow

<Steps>

### Pick a marker

Choose it from the **Marker** dropdown. If the marker is also one of the
image's channels, Plexora puts that channel into the first channel slot so
you can check the threshold against the picture as you set it.

### Set the threshold

Drag either end of the slider, or click a number and type it. The track
shows the marker's distribution, so you can see where the bulk of the
positive and negative populations sit while you place the two ends.

To fine-tune the lower threshold, use the **‹** and **›** buttons in the
top-left corner of the distribution: each click moves it one step down or
up, the same step the arrow keys take with its handle selected. After one
click, the arrow keys keep stepping it, so you can hold one down to move
further.

### Or let Plexora propose one

Press **Auto** to set a threshold from the marker's own distribution. Auto
replaces whatever is on the slider, so the button then reads as the way
back: press it again to put the threshold you had before back.

### Move to the next marker

Press **X** to move to the next marker in the list, **Z** for the previous
one, without reaching for the dropdown.

### Save or export

Gates are kept with the sample as you set them — there is nothing to press
to keep them. Use **Download Gates** or **Save Gates to Source File** when
you want a copy that leaves Plexora; see [What it saves](#what-it-saves).

</Steps>

## Single Cell Classifier

The cog in the card's header switches Thresholding to a second mode, the
**Single Cell Classifier**: instead of setting a threshold, you label a few
cells positive and a few negative, and Plexora calls every other cell from
those examples. Use it when one number per cell does not separate the
positives — a membrane stain whose mean is pulled up by a bright neighbour,
or a speckled stain whose positives are not simply the brightest.

1. Pick a marker, as for a threshold. Every cell is outlined, so there is
   something to click.
2. Press the **wand**. Plexora picks about 40 clear positive and 40 clear
   negative cells spread across the image — from the marker's own
   distribution, away from where positive starts — then trains and
   classifies every cell. Its picks are marked with dashed rings. A positive
   must look positive in its pixels as well as in the table, and not be lit
   mainly by a much brighter neighbour; a third of the negatives are dim
   cells sitting beside bright ones, so the model learns what light spilling
   from a neighbour looks like. Your own labels count twice as much as the
   wand's.
3. Or label cells yourself: press **+ Positive** and click cells on the image
   that are positive; press **− Negative** and click cells that are not. A
   labelled cell turns green or red and gets a solid ring with a plus or a
   minus; clicking it again removes the label. **Esc**, or pressing the
   active button again, stops labelling so the image pans as usual. Then
   press **▶** (Train, at the end of that row) to train on exactly the
   examples there are — at least 3 of each. Your labels are kept when you
   press the wand, which only replaces its own earlier picks.
4. The model learns from each cell's value, its neighbours' values and —
   when the marker is an image channel and the project has a mask — the
   marker's own pixels inside the cell (brightness, edge against middle,
   contrast with the tissue around it, how much sits on the nucleus). After
   a run, positives are outlined in white, uncertain cells in amber and cells
   QC rules out in grey. A cell you labelled is always called the way you
   labelled it.
5. Rest the pointer on a positive or uncertain cell to see its call, its
   score and how many of the resampled models agree. Negative cells show no
   card.
6. Press **Review uncertain cells** to go through up to 8 amber cells,
   spread across the tissue. The view moves to each one and rings it; press
   **P** if it is positive, **N** if it is not, **S** to skip it and **Esc**
   to stop. Then press **▶** to train with your answers. A few answers here
   do the most good: on our own labelled test cells, labelling four amber
   cells after a wand run raised agreement from 72% to about 76%.
7. Add or correct examples and train again: the new run replaces the old
   one. The **eraser** undoes the run and hands the marker back to its
   threshold; the examples are kept.

**Uncertain** is not a fixed band around 0.5. The examples are resampled 25
times and a model is fitted to each draw; a cell is uncertain when at least
a fifth of those models call it the other way. With a handful of examples
that is a wide stretch of cells — label more like them — and it narrows as
examples are added.

The line under the buttons reads **Positive cells: N / M**, where M is the
cells that could be classified, with how many are uncertain. A run is marked
stale if the table, the mask or QC changes afterwards; press the wand to
update it.

**Files.** **Download Cells** offers a binary CSV (one row per cell —
`image_id`, `cell_id`, then a 1/0 column per classified marker, blank where a
cell was not classified), a scores CSV with the model's score in the same
layout (called a probability only once a run had 50 or more examples that you
gave and was calibrated), and the training examples as JSON. **Load Annotations**
reads such a JSON back, reports what matches this sample (unknown cells,
cells labelled both ways, a different mask), and asks whether to **Merge**
it into the examples you have or **Replace** the examples of the markers it
names. **•••** holds **Clear training examples** and **Reset classifier**.

**Save Gates to Source File** also writes the classifier's results:
`layers["plexora_binary"]` (1 positive, 0 negative, −1 not classified) and
`layers["plexora_scores"]` when every classified marker is a var name, or
the same as `obsm` frames when one is not, plus `uns["plexora_classifier"]`
with each image's examples and runs. Only this image's rows are written.

The pixel features are built where the image is: on this machine for a local
image, on the data node for one held there.

## Controls

- **Marker** — which column's values the slider and the picture on screen
  refer to.
- **Threshold slider** — two ends, each independently draggable or typeable,
  over the marker's plotted distribution.
- **‹ ›** — in the corner of the distribution: move the lower threshold one
  step down or up per click; after a click, the arrow keys keep stepping.
- **Auto** — proposes a threshold from the distribution; press again to
  restore the threshold that was there before.
- **Load Gates** — reads a gates CSV (the same shape **Download Gates**
  writes) and applies it to the markers it names.
- **Download Gates** — writes every marker's current range to a CSV, whether
  or not it has been narrowed from the column's full range.
- **Save Gates to Source File** — writes gates into the project's own
  AnnData or SpatialData file; see below. Only offered for a project
  imported from one of those.
- **Cells** (bottom of the sidebar) — how gated cells are drawn: **None**
  (cells off the image), **Centroids**, **Outlines** or **Filled**, plus **Point size** (centroids
  only) and **Opacity**. This row is shared with any other tool that colours
  cells, so it is not part of this card.

## Keyboard shortcuts

The two marker keys are also printed at the foot of the panel.

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Ctrl+B / ⌘B | Open or close Thresholding |
| Z | Previous marker |
| X | Next marker |
| Esc | Stop labelling cells (Single Cell Classifier) |

## What it saves

Every gate you set is saved automatically with the project, in Plexora's own
per-project storage — never in your original file — so reopening the sample
later, or in another session, shows the same gates. Loading a project
imported from AnnData or SpatialData also checks that file's own stored
gates (if any were set outside Plexora) the first time there is nothing
saved in Plexora yet.

Two ways to take gates out of Plexora:

- **Download Gates** writes a CSV with one row per marker: its channel name,
  its lower and upper bound, and whether that range was actually narrowed
  from the marker's full data range. **Load Gates** reads a CSV in the same
  shape back in.
- **Save Gates to Source File** writes the lower bound of every gated
  marker into the project's own `.h5ad` (or SpatialData store), one column
  per image, under the table name you choose (**gates** by default). This
  needs an image id column on record and only appears for a project whose
  source is AnnData or SpatialData — it never rewrites the rest of the
  file.
