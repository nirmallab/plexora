<PluginBadge name="Video Studio" />

Video Studio turns what the viewer shows into a video. Pick a preset — a full
turn of a z-stack, a sweep through its planes, a zoom into a region, the
channels revealed one at a time — watch it play on the image, and export it.
When a preset is not enough, place camera positions yourself and arrange
everything on a timeline.

Video Studio is part of [Plexora Pro](/docs/getting-started/free-and-pro),
and of the 30-day trial.

## When to use it

Use it for a supplementary movie, a talk, or a figure that needs to move: a
3-D structure seen from every side, a stack shown plane by plane, a time
series in order, a tour from the whole slide down to a few cells.

## Requirements

An image. A flat image can zoom, pan and reveal channels; the 3-D and depth
presets need a z-stack, the time-lapse needs a time series, a reveal needs two
or more channels. Presets an image cannot use are listed with the reason.

## Opening it

With a project open, choose **Tools → Video Studio**, press **Ctrl+J**
(**⌘J** on macOS), or click the video camera button at the top right of the
image. The Quick Capture bar appears at the top of the image.

## Core workflow

<Steps>

### Choose a preset

The bar opens on a preset that suits what is on screen: a full turn when the
3-D view is showing, a sweep through the planes for a stack shown as planes, a
time-lapse for a time series, a zoom otherwise. Choose another from the preset
menu at the left of the bar.

### Adjust it

The strip under the bar holds the preset's few settings: an axis and a
length for a turn, the first and last plane for a sweep, a channel order for a
reveal. The video rebuilds as you change them and keeps playing from the same
place.

### Watch it

**Play** (or **Space**) plays it on the image. The scrub rail under the
settings moves through it. The video starts from the view you set up: move
the image or turn the 3-D view, press Play, and it starts from there.

### Export

**Export…** (or **E**) opens the export dialog. Choose a size, a frame rate
and a format, and press **Export**. The viewer is taken over while frames
render — a banner counts them, and **Esc** or **Stop** ends it — then given
back exactly as it was.

</Steps>

## Presets

| Preset | What it does | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| 360° Rotation | A full turn about the stack's X, Y or Z axis, or turning as seen (↔ ↕). Loops without a seam. | z-stack |
| Rock | A back-and-forth turn of ±15°, ±30° or ±45°. | z-stack |
| 3-D Tumble | A turn with a gentle wobble, as a loop. | z-stack |
| Fly-through | The camera moves in toward the stack, turning as it goes. | z-stack |
| Slice Reveal | Planes added to the 3-D view one at a time from the top or bottom, or the stack cut open from a side. | z-stack |
| Z-Stack Sweep | Every plane from one to another at an even pace, up, down or both ways. | z-stack |
| Time-Lapse | Every acquired timepoint in order. | time series |
| Zoom In/Out | From the current view to a closer one (2× to 16×), or to a view you set. | — |
| XY Pan | A steady move across the image, or to a view you set. | — |
| Channel Reveal | Channels fade in one after another, or each alone then all together, optionally with their names. | 2+ channels |

**Storyboards** chain several of these: *Tissue Overview* (the whole field, a
zoom to a region, the markers one by one, back out), *3-D Structure Showcase*
(the stack built up plane by plane, a full turn, a rock), *Sequential Marker
Reveal*, *Orthogonal Exploration* (the plane and its sections swept through
every plane) and *Time-Series Comparison*.

For Zoom and Pan, move the image to where the move should end and press
**Set end view**. The start stays where it was.

## Planes and timepoints are never blended

A frame of a Video Studio video always shows a plane and a timepoint that were
imaged. A sweep shows each plane for an equal share of the time and steps
between them; a time-lapse steps between timepoints. Nothing ever cross-fades
from one plane or timepoint to the next, which would show a picture of
something that was never measured. Colours change only at a keyframe, never
by blending.

What may change smoothly is how the data is shown: the camera, a cut through
the stack, a channel fading in. These are listed as *display transitions* in
the record kept with every export.

## Camera path

**More → Camera path editor** (or **P**) turns the strip into a row of
numbered camera positions.

<Steps>

### Frame a view

Use the viewer's own controls — drag to turn the 3-D view, scroll to zoom,
the axis buttons for a straight-on view — or **Focus on…** to frame a region
drawn with the ROI tool.

### Add it

**+ Add position** (or **K**) adds the view on screen as the next stop. A
preset's own move is kept, muted, rather than lost.

### Refine

Select a position to see it; **Update** replaces it with the view now on
screen. Each segment has its own length and pace, and **Even speed** moves the
camera at a constant speed whatever the spacing of the positions. Over the
image, the positions are drawn as numbered markers along the path (those off
the current view sit at the edge, dashed).

</Steps>

The camera turns smoothly through every position, the same way round
throughout — it never flips back the short way between two views.

## Timeline

**More → Timeline** (or **T**) opens a band along the bottom of the image with
one row per track: the camera, planes, time, each channel, captions. A moving
preset is drawn as a span, keyframes as diamonds.

- Drag a diamond to move it (it snaps to 0.1 s; hold **Shift** to move it
  freely, **Alt** to copy it).
- Click or **Shift**-click to select; **Delete** removes the selection.
- Double-click a diamond to choose how it eases into the next.
- **Capture viewer state** adds a keyframe of everything on screen — camera,
  plane, timepoint, every channel — at the playhead.
- **⌘Z** / **Ctrl+Z** undoes, **⇧⌘Z** / **Ctrl+Shift+Z** redoes.

Plane, time and slab rows are amber: their keyframes hold whole planes and
timepoints, and have no easing.

## Recording an exploration

**More → Record an exploration** records where you take the view — pans,
zooms, turns, planes, timepoints — until **More → Stop recording**. The
recording becomes an editable video: simplified into a few keyframes along
the same route, with long pauses shortened, and opened on the timeline. A
plane you paused on is shown whole; nothing is blended between planes.

## Exporting

| Setting | Choices |
|---|---|
| Size | 1080p (1920 × 1080), 1440p, 4K (3840 × 2160), or a custom size |
| Frame rate | 24, 30 or 60 fps (GIF: 10, 15 or 25) |
| Format | MP4 (H.264), WebM (VP9), ProRes (MOV), GIF, PNG frames (zip) |
| Quality | Draft, Good, High, Max |
| Overlays | Scale bar, plane and time, dataset name, channel legend, axes (3-D), attribution, captions, transition tag (marks frames where a channel or window is fading; on by default) |

Every frame is rendered by the viewer at the video's own resolution — a 4K
video is drawn at 4K, not enlarged — and only once everything in it has
finished loading: the tiles, the sections, the 3-D view's detail, the exact
contrast. A frame is never captured half-loaded.

**Where a video is encoded.** MP4 and WebM are encoded in the browser where
it can (most current browsers); the dialog says so beside each format. ProRes,
GIF and, where the browser cannot, MP4 and WebM are encoded on the Plexora
server with FFmpeg, which the desktop app includes and
`pip install "plexora[video]"` adds elsewhere. PNG frames and short GIFs need
nothing. A format no route can write is listed with the reason.

**Transparent background** is offered for formats that keep transparency
(PNG frames, WebM, ProRes) and frames that are not 3-D.

## What is kept

Each video is saved in Plexora's data folder (`.videos`), separately from any
project. It records the image it was made from, never a copy of it. Every
export writes a `video-manifest.json` beside the file. It records:

- the software and the encoder settings;
- the image, its pixel size and plane spacing;
- the size, frame rate, frame count and duration;
- for every frame, the acquired plane and timepoint it shows;
- every display transition (fades, window changes, cuts through the stack);
- the full tracks, so the video can be made again exactly.

**More → Download project (.plexvid)** saves the video itself as a file.

## Agents

An agent connected to Plexora can make videos too: `create_video` builds a
preset or storyboard from what the viewer shows, `get_video` describes it,
`preview_video` plays it in the open viewer, and `export_video` renders it as a
job; `delete_video_export` removes one export. Edits are reversible operations. Over MCP these tools also need external
MCP access, which is off by default; an administrator can turn it on for your
licence.

## Limits

- A 3-D frame waits for full detail before it is captured, so 3-D exports at
  4K take longest; the export dialog estimates the time before you start.
- A browser-encoded video is held in memory until it is saved; very long 4K
  videos are better encoded on the server.
- A 3-D scale bar is true at the depth of the stack's centre (the view is in
  perspective).
- GIF frame delays are counted in hundredths of a second, so GIFs offer 10,
  15 and 25 fps.

## Troubleshooting

- **The tool says it needs a licence.** Video Studio is part of Plexora Pro;
  see Settings → License, or start a trial.
- **MP4 is greyed out.** Neither this browser nor the server can encode H.264
  here: use WebM or PNG frames, or install `plexora[video]` on the server.
- **An export stopped, naming what it waited on.** A frame did not finish
  loading within the time allowed (30 s, 60 s for 3-D or 4K), even after one
  retry. Nothing unfinished was written into the video; try again, or a
  smaller size.
