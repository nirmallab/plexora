<PluginBadge name="Figure Builder" />

Figure Builder captures views of the image — a region, a zoom level, a set
of channels, whatever another tool is drawing over it — and arranges them
into a figure with scale bars, labels, text, shapes and arrows, which you
then export.

## When to use it

Use it once you have a view worth keeping: a channel combination, a gated
population, a coloured phenotype map, a region of interest. Capture holds
onto exactly what was on screen and where, so you can come back and build
the figure later, or add another panel from a different sample entirely.

## Requirements

Nothing beyond the image. Capturing a field needs only that, so Figure
Builder opens on a project that is nothing but an image, and a figure
itself is not tied to any one project at all — it can hold panels captured
from several samples.

## Opening it

Figure Builder has two doors, for two different things:

- **Capturing** — with a project open, choose **Tools → Figure Builder**,
  or press **Ctrl+K** (**⌘K** on macOS). A dock appears over the image;
  this is where you take views.
- **The figure library** — choose **File → Open Figures…**, or the
  **Figures** tab on the Open Project page (**Ctrl+O** / **⌘O**). This
  works with no project open, and is where you open an existing figure or
  start a new one.

## Core workflow

<Steps>

### Open the capture dock

Press **Ctrl+K** (**⌘K**) with a sample on screen. A small dock appears in
the corner of the image.

### Capture a view

Press the dock's orb, or **C**, to turn on capture mode, then drag a
rectangle over the region you want. Everything currently on screen — the
zoom, the channels and their display windows, any overlay another tool is
drawing — is captured with it.

### Collect what you need

Every capture lands in the dock's bin, whatever image it came from. Click a
thumbnail to fly the viewer back to that region; tick its checkbox to mark
it for the next step. **All** and **None** select the whole bin at once,
and the trash discards whichever captures are ticked.

### Send captures to a figure

Press **Figure Canvas** — it shows how many captures are ticked — and
choose a figure to add them to: an existing one, or a new one. This leaves
the viewer and opens the Figure Canvas.

### Arrange the page

On the canvas, each capture is a panel you can move, resize and re-crop.
Add scale bars, text, shapes and arrows from the tools around the page.
Everything is placed in millimetres, so the page prints true to size
whatever panel it holds.

### Export

Press **Export**, choose a format and a resolution, and download the
result.

</Steps>

<Callout type="info" title="A panel is not a screenshot">
What sits on the canvas is a preview. Figure Builder keeps a link back to
the panel's own source image, its exact region and its display settings,
and re-renders it from the original pixels at export time — at whatever
resolution you ask for, not the resolution the preview happened to be
drawn at. Moving the source file or changing its channels afterwards is
the one thing that breaks the link.
</Callout>


## Controls

**The capture dock, over the image:**

- **Orb** (or **C**) — turns capture mode on or off.
- **Captures strip** — every capture taken from this image. Click a
  thumbnail to aim the viewer back at it; its checkbox marks it for
  inclusion.
- **All / None** — ticks or clears every capture in the bin.
- **Trash** — discards the ticked captures.
- **Figure Canvas** — opens the destination picker for the ticked captures,
  then the canvas itself.

**The figure canvas:**

- **Panels** — each holds one captured view; drag to move, drag a handle to
  resize or re-crop.
- **Scale bar** (per panel, in the image panel's own controls) — length and
  unit (nanometres, micrometres, millimetres, centimetres or pixels),
  corner or edge position, colour, thickness, margin and label size, and an
  eye to show or hide it. It reads the source image's own recorded pixel
  size when there is one; type a pixel size and press the refresh button
  next to it otherwise. An image with no pixel size recorded still gets a
  bar, measured in pixels, rather than none at all.
- **Text** — font (Helvetica, Times or Courier, each regular, bold, italic
  or bold-italic), size, colour, line spacing, horizontal and vertical
  alignment, and bold/italic/underline/strikethrough.
- **Shapes** — seventeen preset shapes (rectangle, rounded rectangle, bar,
  pill, capsule, ellipse, triangle, right triangle, diamond,
  parallelogram, trapezoid, pentagon, hexagon, octagon, five- and
  six-point stars, burst), plus four freeform tools — polygon, curved
  shape, freehand and open path. Every shape's points can be edited
  afterwards.
- **Lines and arrows** — a line, dashed, dotted, an arrow or a double
  arrow; each end's head can be changed independently of the preset you
  started from.

## What it saves

A figure is its own file — a small database under your data directory,
holding the page layout, every panel's placement and crop, and every piece
of text, shape and line, but never a copy of the image pixels themselves.
Edits save automatically as you make them; the corner of the canvas shows
**Saving…** and then **Saved**. Because a figure only holds references, its
source images and their layers need to stay where Figure Builder can reach
them for it to re-render or export.

A capture waits in its image's own bin — kept with the sample, independent
of any figure — until you put it into one from the **Figure Canvas** button.

## Exporting

The export dialog offers three formats:

- **PDF** — text and legends stay editable in the file.
- **TIFF** — flattened, for submission.
- **PNG** — flattened, for circulating.

Resolution is 150, 300, 600 or 1200 DPI. Panels are re-rendered from their
source images at the resolution you ask for, not from whatever is on
screen, and an optional provenance page can be appended recording what went
into the figure. Transparency is kept only in PNG — a transparent page
renders white in PDF and TIFF.

Two limits are deliberate rather than incidental:

- **Twelve fonts.** Text is set in Helvetica, Times or Courier, each in
  regular, bold, italic or bold-italic — the twelve fonts every export
  format can render without a font file, so a figure never fails to export
  over a missing font, and the preview and every exported format agree on
  exactly where a line breaks.
- **16,000 pixels per panel side.** A single panel's rendered image is
  capped at 16,000 pixels on its longest side. A large panel at a high DPI
  can reach this — a 300&nbsp;mm-wide panel at 1200 DPI is already about
  14,000 pixels — and export stops with a message rather than attempting a
  render that would be gigabytes of uncompressed pixels. Below that limit,
  a panel asked to print larger than its source image can supply still
  exports; the dialog reports the effective resolution so a soft panel is
  a decision you made rather than one you find out about later.
