<PluginBadge name="Quality Control" />

Quality Control marks the parts of an image that should not be trusted -- a
tissue fold, a blurred field, antibody aggregates, a failed stain, tissue lost
between imaging cycles -- and, when the sample has a cell table, flags every
cell they affect. Nothing is deleted: each artifact is an ROI you can see and
edit, and each cell gets a pass or fail call with its reasons, which you can
export or write into your own file.

## When to use it

Before gating, clustering or any analysis of a new multiplexed image, and
whenever a result looks driven by a few bad fields rather than biology.

## Requirements

Only the image. A cell table (and a segmentation mask) adds the cell calls:
with a mask, a cell counts as inside a region by how much of it lies there;
without one, by its centre.

## Opening it

With a project open, choose **Tools → Quality Control**, or press
**Ctrl+Shift+X** (**⌘⇧X** on macOS).

## Core workflow

The panel holds three tools, each folded by its header: **Registration QC**
(do the DNA channels line up?), **Segmentation QC** (does the mask hold its
nuclei?) and **ROI QC** (which regions of the tissue are artifacts?). The
panel opens with all three folded and every region hidden, so nothing is drawn
until you ask. A folded tool still shows what it found beside its name, and
its **Run** button runs it.

<Steps>

### Mark a region by hand

In **ROI QC**, click **+** and pick what the artifact is -- or type your
own name under **Custom** and press Enter. You stay in the QC panel: a small
drawing bar appears with the category, and you press and drag round the
artifact on the image. Each region appears under **Regions** as soon as you
let go, and the cells inside it are listed under **Cells**, marked "Derived
from" that region. A category you name yourself is listed with the others from
then on, and counts as a technical artifact wherever QC reasons by artifact
class.

Hold **Space** to move the image while you draw, or switch the bar between
Select/Pan and Draw. Only the regions are drawn at first: click a row under
**Cells** to show those cells, and pick **None** at the bottom of the sidebar
to hide them again. Clicking a region or its cells never changes your image
channels, and never opens the ROI tool. Each region you draw does remember the
channels that were on screen, and its menu can put them back when you ask.
A region you drew is marked **manual**; its menu can rename or delete it.

The regions are ordinary ROIs. Open the ROI tool at any time to rename,
reshape, hide or recategorise them; QC takes the changes in as they are saved.

### Or let an agent check the image

Ask an AI agent to "QC this image" (the robot in the **ROI QC** header copies
those words). It runs a quality-control session: Plexora
scans every channel of the image, the agent looks at every channel and every
suspected region, and each artifact it confirms becomes a region in a QC
category. You can watch it in the viewer and pause, stop or take over at any
time. This needs Plexora Paid; everything else on this page is free.

### How regions are outlined

The agent points at an artifact with a coarse outline on a grid of 50 µm
squares. That outline is only where to look: Plexora then reads the pixels
inside it at about a micron a pixel and traces the artifact itself, the
specks of an antibody aggregate, the bright band of a fold, the blurred or
saturated patch, the dark torn area. It writes that trace with a few microns
of margin, so the normal tissue the coarse outline took in keeps its cells.
The trace always lies inside the outline. When there is nothing to trace, or
the trace would miss the artifact's strongest part, the outline itself is
written. This also happens for seams, shading and failed channels, whose
region is the outline. Each region's notes say which happened.

To retrace a region, choose **Trace outline** from its menu in the QC panel,
or **Trace all outlines** on **Regions**. This works on a region you drew by
hand too. It is undoable, and it needs the image scanned (by a QC session, or
an agent's `profile_image_qc`). A locked region is left alone; unlock it in
the ROI panel first. An agent can do the same with `refine_qc_roi`. To write
the coarse outlines instead, start the session with `refine: false`, or set
`PLEXORA_QC_REFINE=0`. `refine_margin_um` sets the margin.

### Choose how strict

**Lenient** excludes only severe, confident artifacts; **Standard** moderate
and severe ones; **Strict** every confirmed artifact. Changing it re-derives
every region and cell at once -- nothing is asked again -- and regions you
drew, edited or approved keep what you chose.

### Keep what you decided

**Approve** pins a region's action whatever the strictness and locks its
shape. Editing, moving or deleting a QC region in the ROI panel always wins
over what the agent decided.

### Check registration between cycles

**Registration QC** lists the nuclear channels Plexora found (DAPI, DNA,
Hoechst, with any cycle number): the reference at the top, in red, and every
other one under it, in green. Click **Run** to measure every channel against the
reference; each row then shows the share of evaluated tissue displaced by
2 µm or more. The channel being compared sits in channel 2 with the reference
in channel 1, both drawn in their colours. Where the two disagree, and only
inside the blocks displaced past the threshold, zebra stripes crawl over the
pixels, like a camera's over-exposure warning, so aligned tissue never
stripes. **F** turns the stripes off and on. The row's eye stops the
comparison, and another row's eye (or **Z** / **X**) switches to that channel,
turning the previous one off. Channels 3 and up are left as you set them, and
turning the check off puts channels 1 and 2 back.

A colour picked on a row is the channel's colour in **Image Channels** too, and
a colour picked there shows here. Click the reference's name to use another
channel as the reference; the sliders button sets the DNA rule and the
threshold.

The header's two glyphs add two pictures of the mismatch, each on or off,
both scaled to the image you are looking at. The **heatmap** shades the
tissue by how far it moved, from no shift up to this image's 99th-percentile
shift, so even a slight misregistration shows its shape. Its colour bar, under
the rows, works like the transcript density map's: drag either end or type
it (in µm), pick a palette, or press the wand to go back to the automatic
range; the threshold is noted under the bar. The **vector field** draws arrows
in the direction of the local shift, one every few dozen screen pixels at any
zoom, in the heatmap's colours; an arrow of full length is this image's
95th-percentile shift.

### Check the segmentation mask

With a segmentation mask loaded, click **Run** on **Segmentation QC**.
Plexora compares the mask with the DNA stain: one label holding two nuclei is
**Under**-segmented, one nucleus cut across two labels is **Over**-segmented,
each judged against its own neighbourhood so dense tissue is not over-called.
A label is over-segmented only when it holds part of a nucleus whose rest lies
in a neighbour, the two together holding one nucleus's worth of DNA; a dim
nucleus beside a bright one is its own cell, not a fragment. The scale is
measured from the DNA, so whole-cell masks are judged by their nuclei. If most
DNA peaks fall outside the labels (a cell-ring or cytoplasm mask), the row says
**check the mask** and its tooltip explains why.
A progress bar shows while it runs, and you can keep panning. **Under** and
**Over** then show the share of cells flagged (the share of segmented area is
in their tooltips), and each eye turns its cells on or off in the viewer. The
**Under at** and **Over at** sliders change the score a cell needs to be drawn,
for viewing only. The mask's own row has an eye and a colour that draw every
cell of the mask under the flagged ones. The flame shows a **density map** of
where the problems are concentrated, finer as you zoom in, and adds three
categories that exist only on the map: **Large**, **Small** and **Irregular**,
cells far from the mask's own median size or roundness. The sliders button
picks the DNA channel. Without a mask, **Run** asks you to add one.

### Take the results with you

Download **cells.csv** (every cell's call and reasons, and Segmentation QC's
status and scores when it has run), **regions.geojson** (every QC region) or
the **report**, with every percentage stated against its denominator. An agent
can also write the calls into your AnnData, SpatialData or CSV file when you
ask it to.

</Steps>
