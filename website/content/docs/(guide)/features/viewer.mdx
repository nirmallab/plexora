The viewer draws the whole image at every zoom level. A z-stack or a time series adds a depth strip along the bottom, where you switch between one plane, a projection and the stack in 3-D.

<Media src="/media/features/viewer-depth-strip.webp" alt="A z-stack in the viewer with the depth strip along the bottom: buttons for One plane, Projection and The stack in 3-D, the Cut and Crop tools, Sections and a plane slider" caption="A z-stack: the depth strip runs along the bottom of the image." />

## How to use it

<Steps>
<Step>
### Move around

Drag to pan and scroll to zoom. The buttons on the left edge zoom and return **Home** (the whole image). The round button in the bottom-left corner opens an overview map of the tissue.
</Step>
<Step>
### Turn the view

**View › Rotate & Flip** turns the image by 90° steps or any angle and mirrors it. The orientation is saved with the sample. **View › Scalebar** shows or hides the scale bar.
</Step>
<Step>
### Save a view to come back to

Press **M** to save what is on screen: position, zoom, channels and contrast. **Saved views** in the sidebar header opens the tray; click a view to return to it. Saved views are kept by [Figure Builder](/docs/plugins/figure-builder), so they can also go into a figure.
</Step>
<Step>
### Look through a z-stack

On the depth strip, choose **One plane**, **Projection of a range of planes** or **The stack in 3-D** (**V**). Step planes with **]** and **[** (with Shift, ten at a time), and frames with **.** and **,**. **Sections** adds XZ and YZ views beside the image.
</Step>
<Step>
### Open up the stack in 3-D

Drag to turn the stack. **Cut** takes a side off it and **Crop** keeps a box of it (**O** shows or hides the cut panel). **Area** places a square on a plane and opens just that part in 3-D.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Media src="/media/features/viewer-3d.webp" alt="A cropped region of a multiplexed z-stack drawn as a 3-D volume inside a box outline" caption="The stack in 3-D, cropped to a region." />

## Tips

- **HD**, in the sidebar's Cells row, reloads the image at full 16-bit precision for judging faint signal. Leave it off for ordinary browsing.
- In a dataset, **Previous / Next** at the top right (or **B** / **N**) opens the neighbouring sample and carries over your channels, colours, cell display and open tools. Contrast and gates stay each sample's own.
- In 3-D, **X**, **Y** or **Z** limits turning to that axis, and **0** returns to the angled default view.
- A brightfield slide has **Brightness**, **Contrast** and **Gamma** sliders on its Image card instead of channels. They change the display only and are not saved.
