Several tools can draw a colour onto every cell in a sample. This page covers
what they have in common: how a colouring is chosen, how to hide it without
losing your place, and what happens when more than one tool wants to draw
cells at the same time.

## Cell Explorer

[Cell Explorer](/docs/plugins/cell-explorer) is the tool for colouring cells
by any column in the cell table — a phenotype, a cluster label, or a
continuous value like a marker's expression or a cell's area.

- The **colour-by** control at the top of its panel shows the column
  currently drawn; click it to search for another one.
- For a categorical column, a legend lists every category with its colour. A
  search field filters the legend, and **All** / **None** show or hide every
  category at once. Categories you hide there are left off the image, not
  merely dimmed.
- For a continuous column, a colour ramp is shown instead of a legend.
- A column Plexora guessed the kind of — a numeric column with only a few
  distinct values, for instance — carries a **Treat as** choice between
  Categories and Numbers, in case the guess was wrong.
- **Open ROIs**, beside the colour-by control, opens the ROI tool alongside
  Cell Explorer so you can hover a drawn region and see what it is made of,
  broken down by whichever column is on screen.

## Hide the overlay, quickly

Press **T** to hide whatever is drawn over the image — the mask, or a
plugin's coloured cells — and press it again to bring it back exactly as it
was: which layers were showing, how each was drawn, and which cells were
selected. This is a shortcut for looking at the bare tissue and back, several
times a minute, without touching the Cells control at all. It only works from
the canvas (click the image first if a text field has focus), and the key
itself is printed under the sample's name in the corner of the canvas
whenever there is something to toggle — with the caption changing to "Selected
cells hidden" while it is off, since the Cells control keeps showing whichever
mode is set the whole time.

## Stacking cell layers

Thresholding and Cell Explorer can both be open at once, and each holds its
own cell layer. [Thresholding](/docs/plugins/thresholding) supplies no colour
of its own — it restricts which cells are drawn at all, to whichever pass a
gate — so stacking it over Cell Explorer reads as "these are the ones inside
the gate," drawn over the phenotype map underneath. A newly opened tool's
layer goes on top of the stack, and the shared Cells control and opacity
slider always act on whichever layer you opened most recently.
