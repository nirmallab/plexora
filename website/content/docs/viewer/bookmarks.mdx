A bookmark keeps exactly what is on screen — where you are and how far in,
every channel with its colour and contrast, the overlays, and on a z-stack the
plane, the projection, or in 3-D the camera, the crop and the rendering — so
one click brings it all back. It works on any image, in 2-D and in 3-D.

<Callout type="info" title="Bookmarks need Figure Builder">
A bookmark is a [Figure Builder](/docs/plugins/figure-builder) capture with a
bookmark on it, so it is kept with the sample, shows in Figure Builder's
captures marked as a bookmark, and can go into a figure like any other
capture. Where Figure Builder is not installed the pin icon is not shown.
</Callout>

## Bookmark a view

Press **K** anywhere on the image, or **+ Save view** in the tray. The tray
does not need to be open, and K is ignored while you are typing in a field. The view flashes, and its picture flies into the
tray (or into the pin icon when the tray is closed). With reduced motion
turned on, the new bookmark is simply marked instead.

Each bookmark is named **View 1**, **View 2** and so on. Double-click a name to
rename it.

## The Saved views tray

The pin icon in the sidebar header, beside the AI button, opens and closes
the tray along the bottom of the image. Its header shows **Saved views** and
how many there are, with **+ Save view** and a close **×** on the right. Below
is one row of this image's bookmarks, newest first, all the same size, each
with its name.

The row has no scrollbar. Scroll it sideways with the mouse wheel, a trackpad
swipe, or by clicking and dragging; it stops on a whole thumbnail. When there
is more to one side, that edge fades and an arrow appears there.

- **Click** a bookmark to go back to it. Its channels, contrast and view are
  restored and kept, and it is ringed while that view is on screen.
- Hovering over a bookmark lifts it slightly and shows **⋯** and **×** in its
  corners.
- **×** removes the bookmark. The capture stays in Figure Builder, and any
  figure that uses it is unchanged.
- **⋯** offers **Rename**, **Show in Figure Builder** and **Remove bookmark**.

## Back to the previous view

Placing a square on a z-stack opens the stack in 3-D cropped to that square,
and **Crop to area** opens a tighter one. **Back**, the button at the top of
the image, or **B**, returns to the view you came from, smoothly — after a
square, to the plane where you placed it. Your channel and rendering changes
made in the meantime stay as they are. Back only moves the view.

While there is a view to go back to, **B** means Back. Otherwise it is the
dataset's "previous sample" key, as before.

## In the 3-D view of a square

- The view opens at the magnification the plane had, centred on the square:
  it first looks straight down, as the plane did, then turns to the way you
  last looked at the stack.
- The square is a **crop** of the whole stack, and the crop panel comes out
  beside the depth strip. The crop's grips move and resize it at once, since
  nothing has to load. When you stop turning, a finer picture of the part
  kept arrives by itself.
- The stack turns about the middle of the crop, so the square turns in place.
- **O** shows the whole tissue, and again goes back to the crop as you left it.
- The **3-D button** on the depth strip always opens the whole stack, never the
  last square.
- After **Back**, the square you placed is drawn again on the plane while the
  Area tool is on. Turning the tool off — its button or **Esc** — clears it.

After **Crop to area** the crop becomes an area of its own, loaded at the
finest level. Its **centre grip** slides it across the tissue, the same as
Shift+drag, and the grips on its **sides** resize it, the cut staying where it
is.

The Area tool's square keeps the **same size on screen** as you zoom, and a
readout under it says how much tissue it covers. Zoom in to select a smaller
area and out to select a larger one. Set its size under the depth strip's gear.
