A **surface** is a triangle mesh drawn in the 3-D view, beside or instead of the
voxels: the boundary where a channel crosses one value (an *isosurface*), or the
outside of every cell of a 3-D segmentation. Surfaces are layers — each has a card
in the Layers panel, an eye, a colour and a style — and every one records exactly
how it was made.

Surface Studio is part of Plexora 3D, in Plexora Pro. Without it the button and
menu entries are not shown; surfaces that already exist stay readable, drawable,
exportable and removable on any plan.

## Make an isosurface

1. Open a z-stack and press **New surface** — the cubes button in the Layers
   header, **New surface…** in the 3-D view's options menu, or **S** while the
   pointer is over the 3-D view.
2. Choose the channel under **Source**. The dock reads a coarse copy of the stack
   and suggests thresholds — **Otsu**, **Triangle** and **Mixture** — on the
   channel's histogram.
3. Move the threshold. The 3-D view shows a preview surface as you drag, and the
   plane and the XZ / YZ sections show its contour, dashed, so you can judge the
   boundary against the pixels it is drawn from.
4. Choose **Smoothing**, a **Min size** to drop small pieces, and a **Quality**,
   then **Create surface**. Its card follows the build.

The threshold is always in the channel's **raw** intensity units. Changing a
channel's contrast never moves a surface, and the surface's record says which
value it is.

Under **Advanced**: a Gaussian **blur** (in µm) for speckled stains, **background**
subtraction keyed to the diameter of the objects to keep, the **region** (the
whole volume, the area on screen, or the planes of the slab band), a colour and
a name.

## Make a surface for every cell

With a 3-D mask, choose **Cells of the 3-D mask** as the source (optionally just
some cell ids). Each cell gets its own closed surface under its own id — the
same id as its row in the cell table — so colouring, picking and the table line
up. Neighbouring cells that touch are drawn a little apart so they do not show
through each other. The mask itself is never changed.

## Look at surfaces

- **Volume, surfaces or both**: the 3-D view's options menu switches between
  them without reloading anything.
- **Style**: Solid, Glass (see-through), Wire (the mesh's triangles) or Outline
  (a dark silhouette line), with a **Look** (soft, matte, glossy, glass).
- **Colour**: one colour, a colour per object, the cell colours of the active
  tool (Cell Explorer, gating), or a measurement from the Objects table.
- **Clip**: the slab band's planes cut surfaces exactly as they cut the voxels.
- **In the planes**: on the plane view and in the section panes each visible
  surface is drawn where it crosses them, physically aligned with the image.

## Pick, isolate and measure objects

Click an object in the 3-D view (without dragging). Its card shows its id, its
**voxel** measurements from the mask beside its **mesh** measurements, and
whether it touches the edge of the volume. From there: **Hide**, **Isolate**,
**Show all**, **In planes** (the plane through it, centred), and **Neighbours**
within a distance — for cells, with the contact area the two cells share in the
mask.

**Objects…** on the card lists every object with its measurements: sort by any
column, filter to a range, colour by a column, and show only what the filter
keeps.

## Edit and export

Edits — remove small pieces, keep the largest, delete objects, smooth again —
make a new version of the mesh; the previous one is a step back away. **Export**
writes PLY, OBJ, STL or glTF (GLB), in microns when the z step is known, zipped
with a `manifest.json` that says how the surface was made.

## What a surface is, and is not

- An isosurface is the boundary at one value you chose. A nearby value can draw
  a different shape; it is not a validated object boundary.
- Smoothing moves vertices by at most about half a voxel. Mesh areas and
  volumes are close to the mask's, not equal to them: report cell sizes from the
  voxel columns.
- Distances between surfaces are geometry. Two cells that touch are near each
  other; whether they interact is a separate question.
- Without a z step, every size is in voxels and says so.
