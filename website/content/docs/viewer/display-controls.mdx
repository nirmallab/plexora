These controls change how the image looks on screen. None of them change a
pixel on disk, and none of them are a substitute for a better scan.

## Brightfield display adjustments

A brightfield slide — an H&E or IHC scan — has no contrast window to set,
because its pixels are already the final colour the scanner recorded rather
than a quantity to be re-windowed. Instead, its layer card in the sidebar
carries four sliders:

- **Brightness**
- **Contrast**
- **Gamma**
- **Opacity**

Brightness, Contrast and Gamma are a display filter over the drawn image —
nothing is re-fetched or re-decoded, and cell outlines, centroids and ROI
shapes (drawn on a separate layer above the slide) are not affected by them.
A filled segmentation mask, drawn in the same world as the slide, is lightly
affected by these three the way the slide itself is.

Opacity is different: it fades the slide itself while leaving anything drawn
over it — the mask, cell overlays — at full strength, which is the point of
dimming a slide in the first place. It is the same value as the layer's
opacity in the [Layers](/docs/viewer/layers) list.

A **Reset** button beside these sliders returns all four to neutral. None of
the four are saved — they reset the next time you open the page, on the
reasoning that the right place to fix a scan that is too dark is the scan
itself, not a setting that quietly outlives the reason it was made.

## Background colour

Every layer's card has a small colour dot — its **ground**, the colour drawn
behind that layer wherever it has no signal. On the reference image this is
the colour the whole canvas is drawn on; on another layer it is that layer's
own background. Pick **Black** or **White**, any colour from the same palette
[Image Channels](/docs/viewer/image-channels) uses, or leave it at its
default: transparent on a registered layer, and whichever ground fits the
image (black for a fluorescence composite, an off-white for a brightfield
slide) on the reference image.

The ground you choose is also what [exporting the current view](/docs/viewer/export)
draws behind the picture, so a composite you have set to a white ground
exports on white rather than reverting to black.
